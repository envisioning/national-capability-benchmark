# International agenda history discipline

<!-- minimum-entries: 3 -->

This section records institutional changes that help a reader understand how a
country has built, maintained or changed a capability. It is an evidence layer
beside the score. It does not change a score, confidence, frame or ranking.

## Entry floor

An international history section appears only when the country has at least
three entries. The first batch covers Estonia, Singapore and South Korea. The
pipeline reads this document when it generates agendas and refuses to publish a
selected starter country below the floor.

Three entries are the minimum because one case can be an anecdote and two can
be a pair chosen to support a preferred story. Three cases make a small section
that a reader can compare and challenge. A country with fewer than three
verified cases keeps its evidence in the ordinary agenda layer until an
editorial batch clears the floor.

## Entry shape

Each entry carries a name, the year or years, the institutional scope, a source
link and one sentence explaining what the case argues about capability. The
record also carries a published metric, its limits, its status and the
mechanism used to interpret it. The source describes the event or institution;
the editorial sentence is the project's analysis and must not pretend to be
publisher language.

## Source discipline

Use a public-sector source when one exists. An academic source can fill a gap
when the public record is too thin. Press coverage can establish that a change
happened, but it cannot carry the claim about capability by itself. A reader
must be able to reach the source in two clicks from the agenda record.

## Editorial balance

Choose cases that show different kinds of institutional work. A history should
not become a list of successful ministries or a catalogue of one sector. The
three starter countries use the same shape while retaining country-specific
examples:

- Estonia: X-Road, internet voting and the Defence League.
- Singapore: SkillsFuture Credit, public housing and the Centre for Strategic
  Futures.
- South Korea: economic planning, KISA and MyData.

The entries are documented deliveries and institutional changes, not a national
history. They do not imply that the institution is effective in every respect,
that its outcome was caused by one design choice, or that another country can
copy it without the same preconditions.

## Maintenance

Add a country only through an explicit editorial change to the starter list and
with a batch of at least three entries. Refresh the sources during the normal
quarterly editorial cycle. Do not fill a section with a weak case merely to
meet the floor. A stronger replacement can supersede an existing entry while
the record of the editorial choice remains in git.
