# International agenda history discipline

<!-- minimum-entries: 3 -->

This section records institutional changes that help a reader understand how a
country has built, maintained or changed a capability. It is an evidence layer
beside the score. It does not change a score, confidence, frame or ranking.

## Entry floor

An international history section appears only when the country has at least
three entries. The first batch covers Estonia, Singapore and South Korea; the
second adds Germany, the United Kingdom and Uruguay; later batches add the
Netherlands, Chile, Colombia, Japan, Peru, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Panama,
Bolivia, Paraguay, Argentina, the Philippines, Indonesia, Vietnam and Malaysia. The pipeline reads this document
when it generates agendas and refuses to publish a selected country below the
floor.

Three entries are the minimum because one case can be an anecdote and two can
be a pair chosen to support a preferred story. Three cases make a small section
that a reader can compare and challenge. A country with fewer than three
verified cases keeps its evidence in the ordinary agenda layer until an
editorial batch clears the floor.

## Entry shape

Each entry carries a name, the year or years, the institutional scope, a source
link and one sentence explaining what the case argues about capability. The
record also carries a published metric, its limits, its status and the
mechanism used to interpret it. The source describes the event or institution;
the editorial sentence is the project's analysis and must not pretend to be
publisher language.

## Source discipline

Use a public-sector source when one exists. An academic source can fill a gap
when the public record is too thin. Press coverage can establish that a change
happened, but it cannot carry the claim about capability by itself. A reader
must be able to reach the source in two clicks from the agenda record.

## Editorial balance

Choose cases that show different kinds of institutional work. A history should
not become a list of successful ministries or a catalogue of one sector. The
selected countries use the same shape while retaining country-specific
examples:

- Estonia: X-Road, internet voting and the Defence League.
- Singapore: SkillsFuture Credit, public housing and the Centre for Strategic
  Futures.
- South Korea: economic planning, KISA and MyData.
- Germany: Fraunhofer contract research, Kurzarbeit and the THW volunteer
  civil-protection corps.
- United Kingdom: the FCA regulatory sandbox, Sure Start and statutory carbon
  budgets.
- Uruguay: Plan Ceibal, renewable electricity delivery and universal cattle
  traceability.
- Chile: ChileCompra, Start-Up Chile, the 27F earthquake reconstruction and
  the Transantiago transport reform.
- Colombia: 4G road concessions, SECOP II, the digital identity rollout,
  victims' reparations, Fondo Adaptación after La Niña and the Reficar audit.
- Japan: My Number Card, GIGA School, Tōhoku reconstruction, the Fukushima
  decommissioning roadmap and the Monju cancellation.
- Peru: Juntos, Obras por Impuestos, Invierte.pe and the Reconstrucción con
  Cambios delay.
- Costa Rica: FONAFIFO environmental-services payments, the renewable
  electricity system, certified digital signatures, REDCUDI childcare and CCSS
  health-insurance reach.
- Ecuador: dollarization, the Metro de Quito, the 2016 earthquake response,
  electronic invoicing and Crédito de Desarrollo Humano.
- Panama: the Canal's expanded operations, Metro de Panamá, the 120 a los 65
  social pension and drought-era water-and-transit rationing.
- Bolivia: Bono Juana Azurduy maternal and child health transfers, Renta Dignidad,
  Mi Teleférico and the industrial lithium plant's capacity shortfall.
- Paraguay: Tekoporã Mbarete family support, Itaipú binational hydropower,
  SIFEN electronic invoicing and the Ruta PY02 public-private concession.
- Argentina: Asignación Universal por Hijo, the SUBE fare rail, Vaca Muerta's
  state–private energy build-out and the suspended ARSAT-3 satellite programme.
- Philippines: PhilSys, the 4Ps conditional-transfer system, DPWH's national
  infrastructure portal, Typhoon Yolanda recovery and the mothballed Bataan
  Nuclear Power Plant.
- Indonesia: JKN national health insurance, Palapa Ring, Kartu Prakerja,
  InaRISK and the still-incomplete Trans-Sumatra toll-road programme.
- Vietnam: VNeID, near-universal health insurance, the expressway build-out,
  the delayed Cat Linh–Ha Dong metro and Typhoon Yagi restoration.
- Malaysia: MySejahtera vaccination delivery, the Putrajaya MRT Line, the
  terminated Kuala Lumpur–Singapore HSR, flood-warning SMS and JENDELA.
- Thailand: PromptPay, the Universal Coverage Scheme, the Digital ID
  framework, T-Alert cell-broadcast warnings and the terminated Hopewell
  elevated road-and-rail concession.
- Spain: Cl@ve, Kit Digital, the AVE high-speed rail network, La Palma
  volcanic-eruption recovery and the Castor gas-storage closure.
- Poland: mObywatel, Rodzina 500+/800+, Baltic Pipe, Alert RCB and the
  abandoned Ostrołęka C coal block.
- Sweden: BankID, Swish, the 1177 national health portal, the VMA warning
  system, Nya Karolinska and the Barsebäck nuclear-plant closure.
- Ireland: MyGovID, the National Broadband Plan, the HSE COVID-19 vaccination
  programme, NAMA, Met Éireann warnings and the New Children's Hospital cost
  reset.
- Netherlands: DigiD, DigiD Machtigen, MijnOverheid, eHerkenning, Digipoort,
  iDEAL, DigiNotar's trust-chain reset, OVpay, the Omgevingswet and Digital
  Environment System, the PAS nitrogen-permitting reversal, the SER consultation
  model, the national police, NL-Alert, Top Sectors, the Delta Works, the Delta
  Programme, Room for the River, the Betuweroute, the Fyra failure, the
  Groningen gas closure and the childcare-benefits recovery.

The entries are documented deliveries and institutional changes, not a national
history. They do not imply that the institution is effective in every respect,
that its outcome was caused by one design choice, or that another country can
copy it without the same preconditions.

## Maintenance

Add a country only through an explicit editorial change to the selected-country
list and with a batch of at least three entries. Refresh the sources during the
normal quarterly editorial cycle. Do not fill a section with a weak case merely
to meet the floor. A stronger replacement can supersede an existing entry while
the record of the editorial choice remains in git.
