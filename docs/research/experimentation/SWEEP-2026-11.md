# Experimentation source sweep, November 2026

Date: 2026-11-02. Dataset 9.4.0, 125 countries (D153). Track: source-backed
measurement, desk triage. Nothing is wired: no registry row, adapter,
observation or decision changed.

## 1. What this sweep tests

The sweep looks for open sources (D154) that add evidence to Experimentation
across the 125 countries, so that mean confidence reaches 0.40 (O1). It must
not raise the guardrail. D125 is the binding precedent: a source that adds
evidence mostly in richer countries is out, whatever its construct.

Each candidate below has its construct written first (D118): what it
observes; whether that is behaviour, an outcome or a perception; and whether
it is a capability or a condition (D122). Income correlations are printed
where computed, and they decide nothing.

Starting figures, recomputed from `data/out/index.json` and
`data/out/diagnostics.json` (income is `NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD`, 123 countries with
a figure; Cuba and Venezuela have none):

The nine counted rows: patents, trademarks, designs (World Bank, from WIPO),
new public repositories (GitHub Innovation Graph, D145), GEM TEA and fear of
failure (16 countries, D125 hold), and three gaps (`venture_capital_gdp`,
`regulatory_sandbox_activity`, `university_spinouts`).

**Simulation rule.** Confidence is coverage times mean recency times mean tier
quality. A new row raises the denominator from 9 to 10. Recency: two grace
years, then linear decay over twelve, against 2026. The simulation starts from
each country's published `confidenceParts` and reproduces the published mean
(0.392).

## 3. Candidates that pass

### Candidate 1: B-READY business insolvency, operational efficiency (`IC.BRE.BI.P3`)

**Construct (written before the values).** Experimentation needs a way to stop
a failed attempt cheaply. Pillar 3 measures the time and cost to resolve
in-court liquidation and reorganisation in practice, from World Bank expert
consultations. It is not a stock bought with money, and it is not the law on
paper: pillar 1 is the law and pillar 2 the court infrastructure, and both are
rejected above. It is an institutional outcome, not the behaviour of people
trying things. The `failure_tolerance` note already names the cost of exit as
the observable substitute for tolerance of failure. D167 scored the pillar 3
of three other B-READY topics in Agency, Coordination and Trust on the same
reasoning, so this row follows that precedent.

**Licence.** World Bank data catalogue: CC BY 4.0 "allows users to copy,
modify and distribute data in any format for any purpose, including commercial
use", and it "is the default license for all Datasets produced by the World
Bank itself". https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/public-licenses. The World
Bank's B-READY indicator pages list "License : CC BY-4.0". The series is
served in WDI (source 2), last updated 2026-10-08.

**Download.** World Bank API, JSON, no key:
`https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/all/indicator/IC.BRE.BI.P3?format=json&per_page=20000&mrnev=1`.
The existing World Bank adapter reads it.

**Years.** 2025 round only in the API (the second B-READY round). A 2026
round would add countries. I did not verify its date.

**Coverage.** 74 of 125, all at 2025. By quartile: 16, 19, 21, 18. Missing in
Q1 includes Burundi, Ethiopia, Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Haiti, Kenya,
Mozambique, Malawi, Nigeria, Sudan, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe and Myanmar.
Missing in Q4 includes Germany, France, Japan, Switzerland, Norway, Denmark,
Finland, Austria, Australia, the Netherlands and the United Arab Emirates.
Brazil, China, India and Russia are also missing. The holes fall on both
ends, so coverage does not track income.

**Values seen after the construct (printed, deciding nothing).** r with log
GDP per head 0.504 (n 74). Redundancy with the B-READY rows already scored:
dispute resolution pillar 3 (Trust) 0.301, business entry pillar 3 (Agency)
0.415, international trade pillar 3 (Coordination) 0.421, insolvency pillar 1
0.305. All under the 0.85 flag. Eight countries score exactly 0: Angola,
Azerbaijan, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, El Salvador, Laos, Lesotho,
Nepal and the Philippines.

**Effect (simulated, tier 0.95, recency 1.0).**

| Run | Mean confidence | Experimentation confidence r log GDP | Guardrail | Q1 mean confidence |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dataset 9.4.0 | 0.392 | 0.461 | 0.474 | 0.289 |
| B-READY insolvency pillar 3 | 0.409 | 0.456 | 0.452 | 0.311 |

It crosses O1 by itself (0.409) and lowers the guardrail by about 0.02.

**Risks.**
- The zeros. B-READY gives pillar 3 a zero where no proceeding happens in
  practice. That fits the construct (there is no clean way to fail), but a
  zero can also be a data gap. Read the B-READY methodology note on "no
  practice" scoring before wiring, and decide whether zero is a value or
  missing.
- Expert consultation, not administrative records. The same caveat holds for
  the three D167 rows.
- An institution, not behaviour. It observes the exit route, not the people
  using it. If the owner wants only behaviour in Experimentation, this row is
  a check, which publishes it and moves nothing.
- One round. Momentum has no basket for it until a second round.

**Next step.** Preflight with `pnpm bench probe` on `IC.BRE.BI.P3`, then
settle the zero rule in a decision entry and wire it as a new Experimentation
row, class `O`, `higher_better`, through the World Bank adapter. Minor
dataset version.

### Candidate 2: annual export entries, Growth Lab Atlas (BACI as fallback)

**Construct (written before the values).** Each year exporters try new
products and new destinations. Most of these lines are small and many die
within a year or two. Counting the lines started in a year, survived or not,
observes attempts and tolerated failure in the open economy. The data are
transactions, so they count behaviour, not a stock. This is the "self-discovery"
reading of export entry. It is distinct from Adaptability's
`new_export_products_rate` (D149), which counts only products that reached
comparative advantage over fifteen years, that is, successful reallocation.
The proposed row is the annual gross entry, before survival.

Proposed definition, to be fixed in the preflight: new HS six-digit
product-destination lines with exports of at least USD 10,000 in year t and
none in t-1 to t-3, as a share of active lines in t-1, averaged over the last
three years. A share rather than a count per head, so that large exporters do
not win on size alone.

**Licence.**
- Atlas: the Dataverse record for the Growth Lab International Trade Data
  (HS92), version 18.0, carries the licence "CC0 1.0"
  (https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/T4CHWJ,
  read from the Dataverse API on 2026-11-02). This is the dataset the
  benchmark already pins for D149.
- BACI: the CEPII page states "Licence: Etalab 2.0"
  (https://www.cepii.fr/CEPII/en/bdd_modele/bdd_modele_item.asp?id=37). The
  Licence Ouverte 2.0 lets the reuser "share, disseminate, redistribute,
  publish and transmit it" and exploit it commercially, with attribution
  (https://www.etalab.gouv.fr/licence-ouverte-open-licence). The zip files
  download without an account (HTTP 200 on a direct request).
- Both are built on UN Comtrade, whose own bulk terms fail D154. The Atlas and
  BACI publishers release their reconciled data under their own open
  licences, and the project already relies on that for D149. If the owner
  reads the Comtrade origin as a bar, it applies to D149 too.

**Download.**
- Atlas: `hs92_country_country_product_year_6_2020_2024.csv` (2.86 GB) for
  bilateral six-digit lines, or `hs92_country_product_year_6.csv` (1.0 GB) for
  product-only entries, from the Dataverse record above. CSV.
- BACI: `https://www.cepii.fr/DATA_DOWNLOAD/baci/data/BACI_HS22_V202601.zip`
  (301 MB, 2022 to 2024) or `BACI_HS92_V202601.zip` (2.4 GB, 1995 to 2024).
  CSV in zip. Version 202601, released 2026-01-30.

**Years.** To 2024 in both. Recency 1.0 in 2026.

**Coverage.** Both datasets carry every economy through partner reports, so
all 125 have a value by construction, including Cuba, Venezuela and Haiti,
which D149 already reads from partner reports. I did not download the files,
so the count is not verified.

**Effect (simulated, full coverage, tier 0.85 as for the Atlas row, recency
1.0).**

| Run | Mean confidence | Experimentation confidence r log GDP | Guardrail | Q1 mean confidence |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dataset 9.4.0 | 0.392 | 0.461 | 0.474 | 0.289 |
| Export entries | 0.438 | 0.460 | 0.469 | 0.346 |
| Export entries and B-READY insolvency | 0.449 | 0.455 | 0.449 | 0.361 |

A full-coverage row holds the guardrail nearly flat and gives Q1 the largest
gain. The income correlation of the values is not computed. The trade
literature reports higher entry and exit rates for poorer exporters, so the
row may correlate weakly or negatively with income. That is a finding to
print, not a reason.

**Risks.**
- Noise reads as entry. Mirror data, re-exports and misclassification create
  one-year lines that no exporter tried. The value floor and the three-year
  look-back reduce this; the preflight must test thresholds of USD 10,000 and
  USD 100,000 and report rank stability.
- Fragility reads as experimentation. A country whose exports collapse and
  restart gets many entries. Pair the entry share with the one-year survival
  of the lines entered, and publish survival as a check.
- Redundancy with `new_export_products_rate` (Adaptability) and
  `export_diversification`. Measure it in the preflight; above 0.85 the row
  becomes a check.
- Re-export hubs (Singapore, the United Arab Emirates, the Netherlands,
  Panama) start lines they did not make. D149's preflight found their values
  did not move much; repeat that test.
- Resource exporters. A new mineral line is entry; the row takes it, as D149
  does.

**Next step.** A preflight memo under `docs/research/experimentation/` that
fixes the definition, downloads one Atlas file at a pinned version, prints
coverage, rank stability across thresholds, redundancy with the two
Adaptability rows and r with log GDP. Then a decision entry and a new adapter
next to the D149 Atlas adapter. Owner approval for the 1 to 3 GB download is
the first action.

## 4. Rejected and why

- B-READY insolvency pillars 1 and 2, overall score: law and court
  infrastructure on paper, not the cost of exit in practice.
- B-READY market competition pillar 3: mixes competition, innovation and
  procurement services; no single construct.
- World Bank Entrepreneurship Database: the construct fits, but it is already
  scored in Agency as `new_business_density`. Moving it to Experimentation is
  a decision, not a source.
- GitHub Innovation Graph, other metrics: the O1-CANDIDATES verdicts stand;
  the new repositories row is already wired (D145).
- GEM: the D125 hold stands. No GEM release since the 2025/2026 report, so
  the 14 missing poorer countries are still missing. The GEM data page states
  no terms, so D154 is not verified for GEM, including the 16 values already
  scored. That needs its own check.
- UN Comtrade bulk: premium subscription and a portal key. Fails D154.
- WIPO IP Statistics Data Center: forbids republishing its datasets
  (https://www.wipo.int/en/web/ip-statistics/about). Fails D154. This closes
  D126's replacement route (residents' filings at every office).
- Regulatory sandbox lists: they record existence, which D152 moved the row
  away from, and stop in 2020 or 2021; CGAP is silent on terms, Datasphere
  needs an access request, and openICPSR could not be checked (HTTP 403).
- WHO ICTRP and ClinicalTrials.gov: where sponsors site trials. The earlier
  reject stands; the 125 frame does not change it.
- AEA RCT Registry, 3ie, EGAP: where foreign researchers run experiments.
- OECD OPSI cases, OGP commitments: showcases and pledges.
- Wikimedia editors: contribution, not trial.
- OpenStreetMap edits: placed by location of the map, not of the mapper.
- GLEIF LEIs: regulatory compliance.
- FAOSTAT crop items: imputation and a weak construct.
- UPOV: covers treaty members only and skips most of Q1.
- IMF FAS and Findex mobile money: diffusion conditions (D122).
- Findex business borrowing or saving: mostly operating an existing business.
- Global Innovation Index: a composite.
- Commercial startup databases: fail D154.

## 5. Ranked shortlist

1. **B-READY business insolvency, pillar 3.** 74 of 125, even across income
   quartiles, CC BY 4.0, already in the API: it crosses O1 alone (0.409) and
   lowers the guardrail (0.474 to 0.452). The zero rule needs a decision.
2. **Annual export entries from the Growth Lab Atlas (CC0).** Expected 125 of
   125 and a behaviour count of attempts that survive or fail; the largest Q1
   gain (0.289 to 0.346) with the guardrail flat. It needs a definition
   preflight and a large download.
3. **The same construct from CEPII BACI (Etalab 2.0).** A fallback if the
   Atlas bilateral file does not serve the definition.

Not shortlisted but open for the owner: moving `new_business_density` from
Agency to Experimentation, which is a construct decision, not a new source.

## Note added 2026-11-02: candidate 2 rejected

The preflight (`EXPORT-ENTRIES-PREFLIGHT.md`, issue #112) downloaded the
pinned Atlas bilateral file for 2020 to 2024 and rejected annual export
entries as a row and as a check. The entry share covers all 135 and is stable
across thresholds (0.986), but it reads basket size (Spearman -0.93 with log
active lines) and churn, and much of its entry is transit trade. Its top ten
are the smallest exporters. The simulated gain in the table above assumed
admission and does not hold. Experimentation's route to O1 is the B-READY
2026 round.
