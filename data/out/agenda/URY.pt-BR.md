## O que o Uruguai construiu e nenhum indicador conta

Estas são mudanças institucionais documentadas em Uruguai que o framework registra como evidência. Elas aparecem ao lado da nota e não mudam a nota nem a solidez da evidência.

- **Electricity supply rebuilt on renewable sources** (Construção). Uruguay rebuilt its electricity supply between 2010 and 2017 through public tenders and long-term contracts, and in 2025 fossil fuels produced 293 GWh of the 15,855 GWh generated, so 98.2 percent came from hydro, wind, biomass and solar.
- **Plan Ceibal, one connected laptop per child** (Construção). Uruguay gave a connected laptop to every child in public primary school from 2007, the first country to do it nationally, and the household survey found a Ceibal computer in the homes of 35.8 percent of the population in 2018.
- **SNIG, universal individual cattle traceability** (Coordenação). Uruguay required producers to register every calf in a national traceability system from 2006, and by October 2011 the registry covered 100 percent of the herd, 11.75 million live cattle, while identification tags reached more than 45,000 producers each year.
- **AFE passenger rail and network contraction** (Construção). Uruguay's state railway suspended passenger service in January 1988 to focus on freight; by 1990 about half of the national network had been declared non-operational, leaving 1,484 kilometres active from a 2,998-kilometre network, before passenger services returned only partially in 1993.
- **Línea Rivera rail rehabilitation, and its decay** (Construção). Uruguay's transport ministry signed a FOCEM-funded contract in November 2013 to rehabilitate 422 km of the Pintado to Rivera line within 36 months, cut the scope to the 310 km between Paso de los Toros and Rivera in January 2017, took the last provisional handover in May 2019, and by June 2025 reported the line in a degraded state with freight limited to 10 to 25 km/h against its set maximum of 40 km/h and no freight carried in recent years.
- **INEFOP, the tripartite national training institute** (Aprendizagem). Uruguay's Instituto Nacional de Empleo y Formación Profesional, created by Law 18.406 in 2008 and governed by government, employers and workers, grew from 9,696 training, guidance and technical-assistance places in 2009 to 114,364 in 2019, and its director reported that more than 50,000 people took its training in 2023.
- **ANII Alianzas para la Innovación** (Coordenação). Uruguay's national research and innovation agency ANII began co-funding joint academia-firm projects through Alianzas para la Innovación in 2009 and had signed 29 such contracts, committing US$3.85 million and drawing US$1.71 million from firms, by 30 June 2016; in 2023 it approved 23 projects joining the productive sector and academia, matching the 2022 record.
- **Partial and flexible unemployment subsidy, 2020-2021** (Adaptação). Uruguay's labour ministry created partial and flexible unemployment-subsidy modalities by resolution after the health emergency of 13 March 2020, and the social security bank (BPS) paid 12,773 beneficiaries under them in April 2020, while total unemployment-subsidy beneficiaries rose from 45,937 in February to a record 185,769 in May.
- **Plan Nacional de Relocalizaciones, moving households out of flood-prone land** (Adaptação). Uruguay's national housing directorate relocated 2,618 households from flood-prone or contaminated land between 2015 and 2020 under the Plan Nacional de Relocalizaciones and related agreements with departmental governments, against a target of 3,500 to 6,000 households by 2025.
