## What Bolivia built that no indicator counts

These are documented institutional changes in Bolivia that the framework records as evidence. They appear beside the score and do not change it or its confidence.

- **Bono Juana Azurduy maternal and child health transfer** (Adaptability). Bolivia's Bono Juana Azurduy opened its 2024 payment round through 47 authorised financial institutions in all 340 municipalities and six Indigenous territories, tying the transfer to prenatal, delivery and early-childhood health checks; the programme reported 1,525,566 pregnant women and 1,783,936 children reached between 2009 and 2023.
- **Renta Dignidad non-contributory old-age pension** (Adaptability). Bolivia's Renta Dignidad, created by Law 3791 in 2007 and paid since 2008, reached 1,241,440 beneficiaries in 2024 with Bs5.289 billion disbursed through the national social-security administration.
- **Mi Teleférico urban cable-car network** (Building). Bolivia's Mi Teleférico network carried 78,715,401 passenger trips in 2024; its management report compared that result with a 97.7 million annual volume required for budget balance, or roughly 80% of the target while the system remained in service.
- **Industrial lithium carbonate plant below design capacity** (Building). Bolivia's state lithium company doubled lithium-carbonate output to 2,064 tonnes in 2024 after its industrial plant began operating, but the Ministry of Economy reported that technical problems with evaporation ponds and the water-treatment plant kept production below the project's projected capacity of 15,000 tonnes a year.
- **El Mutún steel complex, inaugurated in 2025 and found never to have operated** (Building). Bolivia's Empresa Siderúrgica del Mutún, whose turnkey contract with Sinosteel was awarded in 2016 and whose complex the President inaugurated in February 2025, reported in its 2026 initial accountability review that the project stood at 92% physical progress against 100% accumulated financial execution, that its own supervision found the complex had never entered operation, and that it had served notice of intent to terminate the contract in January 2026.
- **Yo, sí puedo national literacy campaign and post-literacy programme** (Learning). Bolivia's Ministry of Education reports that its national literacy programmes, run as 'Yo, sí puedo' from 2006 to 2008 and continued as a post-literacy programme from 2009, taught more than one million people over 15 to read and write between 2006 and 2017, 74% of them women.
