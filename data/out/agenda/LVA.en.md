## What the indicators miss about Latvia

Documented deliveries linked to missing indicators. They do not affect scores or confidence.

- **Mācības pieaugušajiem, the ESF training programme for employed adults 2017 to 2023** (Learning). Latvia's State Education Development Agency (VIAA) trained almost 77,000 employed adults aged 25 and over through its ESF-funded 'Mācības pieaugušajiem' project between 2017 and 2023, in ten rounds and 2,309 programmes offered by 124 education institutions, with 90 to 100 percent of fees covered.
- **Kompetences centri, the industry competence centres programme** (Coordination). Latvia's six industry competence centres, funded through the Investment and Development Agency with 53.2 million euros of ERDF money, had run 231 research projects involving 164 companies and 33 scientific partners by 31 December 2014, of which 78 were completed and 44 percent involved cooperation with scientific institutions.
- **Ķekava bypass, the first major road PPP in the Baltic states** (Coordination). Latvia's Ministry of Transport and the state roads company signed a design, build, finance and maintain contract for the Ķekava bypass on the A7 on 16 July 2021, the private partner Kekava ABT built the roughly 18 km road from 2021 to 2023 and it opened on 13 October 2023, with the state paying EUR 250.13 million in availability payments over 20 years.
- **Rail Baltica in Latvia, and its cost and schedule overrun** (Building). The Latvian State Audit Office found in June 2024 that the estimated construction cost of Rail Baltica's main line in Latvia had risen sevenfold since 2017 to about 8.7 billion euros, that design work due by the end of 2021 was only 54 percent complete and now planned to finish by the end of 2027, and that the Riga Central Station works contracted in 2019 at 430 million euros had risen to 565 million euros.
- **National warning siren network and its twice-yearly test** (Adaptability). Latvia's State Fire and Rescue Service tested the national early warning siren network on 23 May 2025 and reported that 160 of the 162 sirens tested worked without faults, out of 164 installed, against 119 of 155 in the test of 20 May 2024.
- **Dīkstāves pabalsts, the 2020 downtime benefit** (Adaptability). Latvia introduced a downtime benefit in March 2020 under its COVID-19 emergency law, paying idled employees and self-employed people up to 75 percent of their previous average pay, capped at 700 euros a month, and by 14 August 2020 the State Revenue Service had paid it to 52,867 employees and 2,381 self-employed people, 53.78 million euros in all.
- **Latvija 2050 long-term scenario framework for the State Chancellery** (Anticipation). Latvia's State Chancellery commissioned the University of Latvia under contract 2024/065 of 5 November 2024 to build the analytical framework for the Latvija 2050 strategy; the study, delivered in 2026, set out three macroeconomic development scenarios to 2050 and drew on a Kantar survey in December 2024 and expert focus groups in four regions in September and October 2025.
