## What the indicators miss about Cyprus

Documented deliveries linked to missing indicators. They do not affect scores or confidence.

- **Human Resource Development Authority (HRDA) subsidised training programmes** (Learning). Cyprus's Human Resource Development Authority subsidised more than 106,000 participations in 11,064 single-company and multi-company training programmes in 2024, at a cost of 35.9 million euros, including 68,064 participations of employed persons in standard single-company programmes against 32,987 in 2023.
- **Fourth Highway Project, Larnaca-Nicosia and Larnaca-Kophinou roads** (Building). Cyprus's Ministry of Communications and Works, with World Bank and Kuwait Fund loans effective from August 1986, upgraded the Larnaca-Nicosia and Larnaca-Kophinou roads and rehabilitated 111 km of rural and secondary roads, finishing a year late (loan closing moved from March 1993 to March 1994) at a final cost estimate of US$98.11 million against an original estimate of US$68.9 million, an overrun of about 42 percent.
- **COVID-19 public moratorium on loan instalments** (Adaptability). Cyprus suspended loan instalments by a Minister of Finance decree of 30 March 2020, and by 19 June 2020 banks and credit acquiring companies had suspended the obligations of 50,586 borrowers on facilities with a gross carrying amount of 11.75 billion euros, with 27,184 borrowers already covered by 24 April 2020.
- **Limassol wildfire recovery support scheme** (Adaptability). After the July 2025 wildfire in Limassol district, which the Limassol District Administration recorded as damaging 440 homes, 211 of them destroyed, the Council of Ministers adopted a recovery scheme on 30 July 2025 and the Ministry of the Interior had paid its one-off basic-needs grant to 286 beneficiaries, about EUR 2 million in total, by 13 August 2025.
- **Larnaka and Pafos airports BOT concession to Hermes Airports** (Coordination). The Republic of Cyprus handed both of its international airports to the Hermes Airports consortium under a 25-year build-operate-transfer concession in May 2006; the consortium delivered new terminals at Pafos in 2008 and Larnaka in 2009, and traffic rose from 7,028,959 passengers in 2007 to 13,751,247 in 2025.
