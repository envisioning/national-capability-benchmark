## What the indicators miss about the Dominican Republic

Documented deliveries linked to missing indicators. They do not affect scores or confidence.

- **National 9-1-1 emergency-response system** (Adaptability). The Dominican Republic expanded its National 9-1-1 Emergency and Security System to 27 provinces by July 2026, covering 94.64% of the population, or more than 9.6 million people.
- **INFOTEP national technical and vocational training** (Learning). The Dominican Republic's tripartite training institute INFOTEP, created by Law 116-80 in 1980, registered 1,027,791 participants in training actions nationwide in 2025 and 3,613,098 participants between August 2020 and August 2025.
- **Quédate en Casa emergency cash transfer** (Adaptability). Announced on 25 March 2020 and paying from April, the Dominican government's Quédate en Casa transfer was built on the existing SIUBEN registry and PROSOLI payment system and, by the president's report to the Senate of 10 July 2020, reached more than 1.6 million households, 780,000 of them new to social assistance.
- **Punta Catalina thermoelectric plant, and its cost overrun** (Building). The Dominican state electricity holding CDEEE awarded the Punta Catalina coal plant in November 2013 and agreed a final contract price of US$1,945.0 million after the award, including the unloading port; after construction delays and a March 2020 dispute settlement with the contractor, the value carried into the plant's 2022 public trust was US$2,340.54 million, about 20 percent above the contract price.
- **Plan Nacional de Viviendas Familia Feliz, and its intake freeze** (Coordination). By May 2024 the Dominican government's Familia Feliz plan, in which private developers build low-cost homes and the state pays down-payment, tax and mortgage-rate subsidies, had built 3,979 homes and assigned RD$1,108 million in subsidies.
