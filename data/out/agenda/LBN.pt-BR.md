## Com o que o Líbano conta

As condições descrevem com o que um país conta: infraestrutura, acesso, dinheiro, pessoas, matrículas e a própria renda. Cada uma aparece ao lado de uma capacidade e não entra na pontuação, na solidez da evidência nem na tendência. Lidas ao lado da pontuação, permitem ver se o que o país tem se converte no que ele faz. A posição conta os países com valor, do melhor para o pior.

## O que os indicadores não captam sobre o Líbano

Entregas documentadas ligadas a indicadores ausentes. Elas não alteram as pontuações nem a solidez da evidência.

- **Emergency Social Safety Net (AMAN), and its slow start** (Adaptação). Lebanon's emergency cash transfer for the economic collapse that began in 2019 was approved by the World Bank in January 2021, rewritten by Parliament in March 2021 and declared effective only in July 2021, so that in October 2021 the number of households receiving basic cash transfers still stood at its 15,153 baseline against a target of 147,000; the programme later reached 158,770 households by March 2025.
- **COVID-19 vaccination campaign** (Adaptação). Lebanon's health authorities reported to WHO that 2,740,227 people, 40 per 100 population, had received at least one COVID-19 vaccine dose by 22 December 2022, from a first dose given on 14 February 2021, with 2,414,223 (35 per 100) completing the primary series.
- **Greater Beirut Water Supply Project (Awali to Beirut conveyor), and its unfinished delivery** (Construção). Lebanon's Council for Development and Reconstruction and the Beirut Mount Lebanon Water Establishment built most of a system to bring 250,000 cubic metres a day from the Awali to 1.2 million people in Greater Beirut, but when the World Bank loan closed on 30 April 2025, 106 months after the original June 2016 closing date, no additional water had been delivered, the 24/7 supply reached in a 20,000-customer pilot zone in 2018 had not been maintained, and component costs had risen from US$370 million at approval to US$561.7 million.
- **Private management of the state mobile networks, and its end** (Coordenação). Lebanon's government kept ownership of its two mobile networks, Alfa and Touch, and paid private operators to run them under management contracts from 2004, and mobile subscriptions grew from 884,445 in 2004 to 4,237,962 in 2019, the last full year of private management; the government did not renew the contracts in September 2020 and took management back itself.
